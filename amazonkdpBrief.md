## 2. Amazon Book Description (HTML Formatted for KDP Description Box)

```html
<h3><strong>Stop Memorizing Philological Tables. Start Compiling Classical Sanskrit.</strong></h3>

<p>For over two centuries, students approaching Classical Sanskrit have been subjected to an intimidating gauntlet of 800-page 19th-century philological grammars—confronted with hundreds of arbitrary inflectional tables, confusing vowel mutations, and dry academic exercises disconnected from actual epic literature.</p>

<p><strong>There is a vastly superior way.</strong></p>

<p>Twenty-four hundred years ago, in ancient India, the master grammarian <strong>Pāṇini</strong> created the <em>Aṣṭādhyāyī</em>: a 3,959-rule computational algorithm that modern computer scientists recognize as the world’s first generative compiler, functional term-rewriting system, and context-free grammar engine.</p>

<p>In <strong><em>Sanskrit by Architecture: Decoding the Rāmāyaṇa</em></strong>, engineer and researcher Timothy A. George reconstructs Classical Sanskrit from the ground up through the lens of modern software architecture, formal language theory, and physiological vocal-tract mechanics.</p>

<hr />

<h3><strong>Inside This Architecture-First Blueprint:</strong></h3>

<ul>
  <li><strong>The "Base + Badge" Nominal System:</strong> Deconstruct the 7-case nominal routing network (<em>Kārakas</em>) not as static tables to memorize, but as dynamic argument pointers dispatched to verbs.</li>
  <li><strong>The Kinetic State Machine:</strong> Model verbal roots (<em>Dhātus</em>) through 10 functional execution classes, mastering present, imperfect, imperative, and optative modes.</li>
  <li><strong>Physical Mouth Mechanics (Sandhi):</strong> Learn why sounds collide and mutate through the literal biomechanics of the human vocal tract (lip closure, tongue positioning, nasal resonance) without confusing phonetic jargon.</li>
  <li><strong>The Script Gate:</strong> Cross the threshold into reading genuine Devanāgarī script in Chapter 6 using intuitive conjunct anatomy and ligature breakdown.</li>
  <li><strong>The Relative-Correlative Engine:</strong> Master the workhorse syntax of Epic poetry—the <em>Yat-Tat</em> pointer loop that powers classical Sanskrit discourse.</li>
  <li><strong>Epic Combat Capstones:</strong> Step-by-step morphological and grammatical deconstructions of the climactic battle scenes of Vālmīki’s <em>Rāmāyaṇa</em> (Rāma vs. Kumbhakarṇa in Sarga 67, the Final Arrow to Rāvaṇa in Sarga 108), concluding with autonomous synthesis of the <em>Bhagavad Gītā</em>.</li>
</ul>

<hr />

<h3><strong>Open-Source Companion Toolchain:</strong></h3>
<p>This book is backed by an open-source GitHub companion repository featuring:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Scala 3 Generative Compiler:</strong> Runnable computational models implementing Pāṇinian bitmasks and term-rewriting engines.</li>
  <li><strong>Python 3 Transliteration Reader:</strong> Lossless IAST to Devanāgarī converters and dictionary memory loaders.</li>
  <li><strong>Official Errata & Reader Discussions:</strong> Live community Q&A and technical addenda at <code>om-script.github.io/sanskrit-by-architecture</code>.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Whether you are a programmer fascinated by the computational origins of language, an indie scholar seeking to read the Epics in their original tongue, or a student tired of rote memorization, <em>Sanskrit by Architecture</em> delivers the architectural key to decode Classical Sanskrit.</strong></p>
```
